Passage A: The Honeybee Mystery

Honeybees are among the most industrious insects on Earth. They visit thousands of flowers every day, collecting nectar to make honey and transferring pollen from flower to flower. This process, known as pollination, is essential for the growth of fruits, vegetables, and flowers.

Recently, scientists have noticed a worrying decline in bee populations worldwide. Threats such as habitat loss, pesticide use, and changing climate patterns are harming these vital pollinators. Without bees, many crops would fail, leading to severe impacts on our global food supply.

Passage B: Solar Energy in Schools

More schools around the globe are installing solar panels on their roofs to generate clean, renewable electricity. Solar panels turn sunlight directly into energy, reducing reliance on fossil fuels and lowering utility bills for schools.

In addition to saving money, solar installations serve as living laboratories for students. Science classes can observe real-time energy production and learn about environmental conservation firsthand.
